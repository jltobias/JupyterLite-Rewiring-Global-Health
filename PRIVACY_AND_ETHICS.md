# Privacy, Geoprivacy, AI Ethics, and Do-No-Harm

## Principles

1. **Purpose limitation** — define the public-health decision before collecting or linking data.
2. **Minimum necessary data** — reduce attribute and spatial precision where possible.
3. **Geoprivacy by design** — exact location can be identifying even without names.
4. **Synthetic-by-default teaching** — public labs should not need real sensitive patient data.
5. **Human agency** — AI recommends or assists; accountable people make consequential decisions.
6. **Uncertainty is visible** — maps, models, and digital twins must expose assumptions and limitations.
7. **Equity review** — evaluate missingness, representation, performance, and harms across populations and places.
8. **Secure credentials** — never place long-lived secrets in a public notebook, repository, or browser storage.
9. **Output review** — derived maps, logs, screenshots, and labels can leak information.
10. **No automation is also an option** — some tasks should remain manual or should not be performed.

## Public vs. operational boundary

The examples here are research/education prototypes. Operational deployments must follow applicable legal, privacy, security, records-management, ethics, and organizational requirements.
